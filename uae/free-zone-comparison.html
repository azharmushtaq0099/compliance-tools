Guide

How to Choose the Right UAE Free Zone

With over 40 UAE free zones, choosing the right one depends on four factors: your business activity, budget, visa requirements, and whether you need a Dubai address. This guide covers the 12 most popular and accessible free zones for 2024.

Budget-conscious businesses

RAKEZ, Ajman Free Zone, and Fujairah Creative City offer the lowest setup costs while maintaining good UAE banking access. RAKEZ is the most recommended budget option for businesses that need credibility with UAE banks. Ajman Free Zone is cheapest overall (from AED 6,000) and can complete setup in as little as one week.

Dubai address on a budget

IFZA offers the best value for a genuine Dubai address, with licenses starting around AED 12,900. It accepts a wide range of business activities including consultancy, trading, and technology, and processes applications within 1–2 weeks. It consistently ranks as the fastest-growing free zone in Dubai.

Premium trading and tech businesses

DMCC is the world's top-ranked free zone for five consecutive years and is the go-to choice for trading, commodities, and crypto businesses. It has the most comprehensive business ecosystem and is particularly favoured for crypto/Web3 companies due to its Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) licensing framework. Costs start around AED 22,000.

Dubai Internet City is the premier address for tech companies — home to Google, Microsoft, IBM, Oracle and 1,600+ technology firms. If your ICP includes enterprise tech clients, a DIC address signals credibility that no other free zone matches.

Regulated industries (financial services)

Financial services firms must use DIFC (Dubai) or ADGM (Abu Dhabi). Both operate under English common law and are globally recognised by financial regulators. DIFC has its own financial regulator (DFSA) and courts. Costs are significantly higher (AED 40k+) and setup takes 6–12 weeks due to regulatory approvals — but there is no alternative for licenced financial services.

Understanding UAE corporate tax (2023)

Since June 2023, both mainland and free zone companies are subject to 9% UAE corporate tax on profits above AED 375,000 per year. Free zone companies that meet the "Qualifying Free Zone Person" criteria can still access 0% corporate tax on qualifying income. Consult a UAE-registered tax advisor before choosing your structure.

Data accuracy: All cost and setup time data is sourced from official free zone authority websites and is updated monthly. These are indicative ranges — actual costs depend on the specific license package, number of activities, and any promotional pricing from the free zone. Always confirm current pricing directly with the free zone authority before making a decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ajman Free Zone is generally the lowest-cost UAE free zone, with license costs starting from approximately AED 6,000–12,000. Fujairah Creative City is similarly affordable at around AED 5,750. RAKEZ (Ras Al Khaimah Economic Zone) is the most affordable major free zone with good banking support, starting from AED 7,500. For a Dubai address, IFZA offers the best value starting around AED 12,900.
A UAE free zone company offers 100% foreign ownership, zero import/export duties within the free zone, and no corporate tax on qualifying income. Free zone companies were traditionally restricted from directly trading with UAE mainland customers without a local distributor. Following the 2023 UAE corporate tax law, both structures are now subject to 9% corporate tax on profits above AED 375,000, with free zones qualifying for 0% on qualifying income under specific conditions.
The number of visas depends on the free zone and your office arrangement. Flexi-desk packages typically allow 2–3 visas, standard office packages allow 4–6 visas, and larger offices can accommodate more. DMCC, JAFZA, DIFC and ADGM offer higher visa quotas for premium packages. RAKEZ and Ajman Free Zone typically allow up to 5–6 visas per standard license.
IFZA (International Free Zone Authority) is a Dubai free zone that has become one of the most popular for small businesses and consultancies due to its combination of a Dubai address, competitive pricing (starting around AED 12,900), fast processing (1–2 weeks), flexible business activities and good banking support. It offers up to 6 visas per standard package and accepts a wide range of activities including consultancy, trading, and technology.
DMCC (Dubai Multi Commodities Centre) is the UAE's most prestigious free zone, top-ranked in the world for five consecutive years. It is best for trading companies, commodities businesses, precious metals traders, cryptocurrency firms, and companies wanting the most prestigious Dubai address. License costs start around AED 22,000. DMCC is particularly favoured for crypto/Web3 businesses due to its Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) licensing framework.
Setup times vary by free zone. The fastest (Ajman, IFZA, RAKEZ) can complete licensing in 5–10 working days. Mid-tier free zones like DMCC and Dubai Silicon Oasis typically take 3–4 weeks. Regulated free zones like DIFC and ADGM can take 6–12 weeks due to regulatory approvals. All free zones require a valid passport, completed application forms, and payment of fees.
Most UAE free zones offer flexi-desk options (shared workspace) as the most affordable choice, which satisfies the office requirement. Standard packages include a dedicated desk or small office. DIFC and ADGM typically require physical office space due to their regulated nature. For virtual/remote businesses, flexi-desk packages in IFZA, RAKEZ and Ajman are the most popular options.
Traditionally, free zone companies could not directly trade with UAE mainland customers without appointing a local distributor or agent. However, the UAE government has been relaxing these rules. Many free zones now offer permits to trade with the mainland. The specific rules depend on the free zone and business activity — check with your chosen free zone before assuming direct mainland trading is permitted.
