Calculation methodology

How is holiday entitlement calculated in the UK?

The minimum statutory holiday entitlement in the UK is set by the Working Time Regulations 1998. Every worker is entitled to 5.6 weeks' paid annual leave per year. For a worker who works 5 days a week, this equals 28 days — the statutory maximum for full-time workers.

Full-time and part-time workers

The formula is straightforward: 5.6 × days worked per week, capped at 28 days. Part-time workers receive a pro-rata equivalent. For example, someone working 3 days per week is entitled to 5.6 × 3 = 16.8 days per year. Entitlement cannot be less favourable for part-time workers than for comparable full-time workers.

  • 5 days/week (full-time): 5.6 × 5 = 28 days
  • 4 days/week: 5.6 × 4 = 22.4 days
  • 3 days/week: 5.6 × 3 = 16.8 days
  • 2 days/week: 5.6 × 2 = 11.2 days

Irregular-hours and zero-hours workers (2024 rules)

From 1 January 2024, the accrual method for irregular-hours and part-year workers became the statutory standard. Holiday accrues at 12.07% of hours actually worked in each pay period. The 12.07% rate is derived from 5.6 ÷ (52 − 5.6) = 5.6 ÷ 46.4.

Employers may choose to pay rolled-up holiday pay — adding the 12.07% supplement directly to each payslip rather than separately when leave is taken. This became lawful for irregular-hours workers from January 2024 under the Employment (Amendment of Retained EU Law) Act 2023, but it must be clearly identified as a separate line on the payslip.

Bank holidays

There is no automatic legal right to paid bank holidays on top of the 28-day statutory minimum. Employers may count bank holidays as part of the 28-day entitlement. Whether bank holidays are additional paid leave depends entirely on what the employment contract says.
