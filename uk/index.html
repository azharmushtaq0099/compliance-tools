What is the Employment Rights Act 2024?

The Employment Rights Act 2024 is the most significant overhaul of UK employment law in a generation. It received Royal Assent in October 2024 and introduces sweeping changes to worker rights, employer obligations, and collective bargaining rules. Different provisions take effect on different dates through 2024–2027.

Key changes under ERA 2024 include:

  • Zero-hours guaranteed hours: Workers on zero-hours or variable-hours contracts who work regular hours for 12 consecutive weeks can request a guaranteed-hours contract — expected in force 2026
  • Day-one unfair dismissal protection: The two-year qualifying period for unfair dismissal will be removed, with a probationary period regime to replace it — expected in force 2026
  • Flexible working as default: Workers gain the right to request flexible working from day one of employment (already in force, April 2024)
  • Neonatal Care Leave: Up to 12 weeks of paid leave for parents of babies requiring neonatal care — in force April 2025
  • Third-party harassment duty: Employers must take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment by third parties such as clients and customers — in force October 2024
  • Fire and rehire restrictions: Dismissal to re-engage workers on worse terms is made automatically unfair except in the narrowest circumstances — in force 2024
  • Collective redundancy reform: The 20+ dismissal threshold for collective consultation is now calculated across all of an employer's sites, not per establishment
  • Strengthened trade union rights: Simplified recognition procedures, right of access to workplaces, electronic balloting permitted
