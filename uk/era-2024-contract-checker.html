About ERA 2024

What is the Employment Rights Act 2024?

The Employment Rights Act 2024 (ERA 2024) is the most significant piece of UK employment legislation in over 30 years. Receiving Royal Assent in October 2024, it introduces sweeping changes to employment rights with a phased implementation schedule running from 2024 to 2026.

The Act covers eight main areas: zero-hours contract rights, flexible working, pregnancy and maternity protection, neonatal care leave, harassment prevention, fire-and-rehire restrictions, collective redundancy, and day-one unfair dismissal.

Implementation timeline

  • April 2024: Day-one right to request flexible working
  • October 2024: Employer duty to prevent third-party sexual harassment
  • In force: Fire-and-rehire automatically unfair dismissal restrictions
  • In force: Group-wide collective redundancy consultation thresholds
  • April 2025: Neonatal Care Leave (up to 12 weeks, day-one right)
  • 2026 (expected): Zero-hours guaranteed hours right after 12 weeks
  • 2026 (expected): Day-one unfair dismissal protection

How does the compliance checker work?

The checker covers the seven compliance areas most relevant to employers' current contracts and policies. For each area, you select the option that best describes your current position. The tool calculates a compliance score (0–100%) and produces a breakdown showing which areas are fully compliant, which need updating, and which require immediate action.

The scoring is weighted: areas already in force (flexible working, harassment prevention, neonatal leave) carry full weight. Areas with imminent implementation (zero-hours, day-one dismissal) are included with appropriate weighting to allow for advance preparation.

Important: This tool provides general guidance based on the Employment Rights Act 2024 as enacted and ACAS published guidance. It does not account for your specific contractual arrangements, workforce demographics, or sector-specific rules. Always consult a qualified employment law solicitor for advice specific to your organisation.

What action should I take after running the checker?

For any area marked as fail (red): these represent current non-compliance with provisions already in force. Prioritise these immediately. Tribunal risk is live.

For areas marked as warn (amber): these are partially compliant or represent provisions coming into force soon. Schedule these for the next HR policy review cycle.

For areas marked as pass (green): your current approach meets the requirement. Review again when secondary legislation for 2026 provisions is published.

Free official resources: ACAS.org.uk and GOV.UK Employment Rights Bill.

The Employment Rights Act 2024 received Royal Assent in October 2024. Different provisions come into force on different dates. The day-one flexible working right took effect April 2024. Third-party harassment prevention duty took effect October 2024. Neonatal Care Leave became law from April 2025. Zero-hours guaranteed hours rights and day-one unfair dismissal protection are expected in 2026.
Under ERA 2024, workers on zero-hours or variable-hours contracts who work regular predictable hours for 12 consecutive weeks will have the right to request a guaranteed-hours contract reflecting those hours. Employers must offer this contract or provide valid business reasons. This applies to agency workers too. Expected implementation: 2026.
Yes. ERA 2024 applies to all UK employers regardless of size. Small businesses must comply with flexible working day-one rights (in force), neonatal care leave (in force April 2025), and enhanced harassment prevention duties (in force October 2024). Zero-hours provisions will apply to businesses of all sizes when they come into force.
ERA 2024 makes dismissal for the purpose of re-engaging an employee on worse terms automatically unfair, except in very narrow circumstances where the employer faces genuine financial difficulties threatening business survival, has genuinely consulted with trade unions or employee representatives, and the changes are necessary to address those difficulties.
Since October 2024, employers must take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment of their workers by third parties such as customers or clients. The EHRC can take enforcement action. Employers should have a written harassment prevention policy, provide training, and have clear reporting procedures.
Neonatal Care Leave is a new statutory right in force from April 2025. Parents of babies who require neonatal care (hospitalisation) within the first 28 days of life for at least 7 days are entitled to up to 12 weeks of paid neonatal care leave. This is in addition to maternity, paternity and shared parental leave. It is a day-one right.
ERA 2024 extends collective redundancy consultation obligations so that the 20+ redundancy threshold is calculated across an employer's entire operation, not just a single establishment. Previously, an employer could dismiss 19 workers at each of multiple sites without triggering collective consultation. Now total dismissals across all sites are counted.
